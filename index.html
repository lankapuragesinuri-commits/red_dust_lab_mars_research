01 THE RESEARCH QUESTION PROJECT OVERVIEW

A SMALL PARTICLE. A CRITICAL ENERGY SYSTEM.

Studying solar
performance on a
dust-active world.

Solar-powered exploration systems depend on light reaching photovoltaic surfaces. On Mars, dust can remain suspended in the atmosphere and can also accumulate on solar arrays. Both processes matter, but they are not the same physical effect.

This project records the voltage response of three photovoltaic units while a prepared dust analogue was introduced into a transparent chamber illuminated by an artificial light source.

How does exposure to Martian dust analogue in a transparent chamber affect the measured voltage output of three photovoltaic units under artificial illumination?
RESEARCH SCOPE

This is a preliminary Earth-based analogue experiment. It does not reproduce Mars' low-pressure carbon-dioxide atmosphere, gravity, temperature, natural sunlight or wind conditions.

06 MARS CASE STUDY MERIDIANI PLANUM

A REAL SOLAR-POWERED ROVER REGION

Opportunity's
dust story at
Meridiani Planum.

Meridiani Planum is a relevant case-study location because NASA's Opportunity rover operated there for more than seven Martian years. NASA's Mars Exploration Rover power-operations report describes a seasonally dependent pattern of gradual dust accumulation and removal at this site.

The mission team developed a Dust Factor to track how much sunlight penetrated dust accumulated on the solar array. A Dust Factor of 1.0 represented a clean array. This is a useful scientific precedent for studying dust and solar energy, although RED DUST LAB does not calculate a Mars Dust Factor.

08 SCIENTIFIC METHOD & LIMITATIONS INTERPRETATION

A RESULT IS ONLY AS STRONG AS ITS METHOD

What the data
can—and cannot—say.

A careful preliminary result is more credible than a confident conclusion that the experiment cannot yet support.

01

Hypothesis

Increasing dust exposure was expected to reduce light reaching the photovoltaic surfaces and therefore reduce their measured electrical response.

02

Observed outcome

Measured voltage decreased during the observation period. At 3:06 p.m., Rover 01 and the main panel were reported at 0 V / no measurable output; Rover 02 retained approximately 3.5 V.

03

Preliminary interpretation

The trend is consistent with the hypothesis, but this single run cannot isolate dust as the only cause of the voltage changes.

04

Further investigation

Repeat trials, a dust-free control, measured light intensity, a defined electrical load, panel-temperature records and measured deposited mass would strengthen the analysis.

FUTURE PHASE / 01

Can panel performance be recovered?

A separate, supervised follow-up could compare pre- and post-cleaning measurements under the same illumination and electrical load. No cleaning or recovery trial is included in the current dataset.

FUTURE PHASE / 02

Separate dust from illumination effects

Measure light intensity before and after the chamber, then conduct a separate lamp-intensity study. This would help distinguish reduced illumination from other changing experimental conditions.
